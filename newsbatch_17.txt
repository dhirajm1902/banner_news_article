The decision by the main western Canadian ski provinces of Alberta and British Columbia (BC) to end seasonal clock changes and adopt permanent daylight time is already having an impact on their ski resorts, with a growing number announcing revised operating hours for the upcoming 2026-27 season, particularly in the darkest months of midwinter. Both provinces will no longer turn clocks back in November, meaning darker winter mornings but lighter afternoons. The change has prompted ski operators to adjust schedules to better align with available daylight and mountain safety requirements.  Most will open and close half-an-hour later than previously, keeping the ski day the same length, but slightly later in the day. Those in BC so far announcing that’s the plan from when they open next month include North America’s largest, Whistler Blackcomb , and Canada’s second biggest, Sun Peaks . In the Banff region of Alberta, Sunshine Village and Lake Louise Ski Resort , where the planned season start is just a fortnight away, Mount Norquay and Nakiska have all confirmed that lifts will begin running at 9:30am this winter, half an hour later than traditional opening times. While Nakiska plans to operate lifts until 4pm, Lake Louise Ski Resort , Sunshine Village and Mount Norquay will remain open until 4:30pm, extending afternoon skiing opportunities. Operators say the later starts are necessary because permanent daylight time will push winter sunrises significantly later.
